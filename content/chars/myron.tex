\subsubsection{Background}

Smaud Myron (real first name: Smord, which he does not advertise) is a gnome Arbiter --- one of the Church's elite warrior-enforcers, trained in both martial combat and divine justice.  Older and more experienced than most of his companions, Myron carries the weight of a complicated past.  Decades ago, he participated in the purge of the Book Burners, an act that placed him at odds with the very people he would later fight alongside.

His father, Professor Jorge Myron, is a gnome tinker with a Cockney accent who teaches at Linderdorf University.  His mother left the family when Myron was young, joining a cult that was subsequently destroyed by the Church --- an irony not lost on Myron, who enforced the Church's will for decades.  Jorge later remarried Holly Dew Thistlebloom, a halfling businesswoman whose own father was an ex-Book Burner killed in the purge that Myron helped carry out.

Myron joined the party after a dramatic entrance involving a hot air balloon crash near the Tower of Ruh'Breks, bringing with him a level of tactical competence and martial skill that the group sorely needed.

\subsubsection{Personality and Traits}

Myron is serious, disciplined, and exceptionally skilled in combat.  He wields a gun with bayonet --- a combination weapon that allows him to fight at both range and in close quarters with devastating efficiency.  In battle, he is described as ``awesome'' with alarming frequency, sliding underneath enemies, slashing their underbellies, and generally being the most competent fighter the group has ever seen.

Outside of combat, Myron has a softer side.  He purchases personalised robes for the entire party, makes beef jerky as a comfort food, and bought a travel ping pong table that provided ``the happiest moment'' of Exme's life when she turned out to be a natural.  He expresses doubt about magical solutions (such as teleportation circles) but follows through with quiet professionalism.

\subsubsection{Relationships}

Myron and Exme have been involved in an ongoing ``will they, won't they'' relationship that Exme herself documented in a self-published novelette entitled ``Love Doesn't Have a Height Limit''.  Their connection deepened over the course of the Masuda campaign, with Myron caring deeply about Exme's safety and Exme becoming ``extremely jealous'' in the presence of other women near Myron.  That connection shattered after Kolo's rampage in the Hope's Rest sewers.  Myron charged in first to fight the mutated Kolo, took an arrow barrage doing so, and went down hard.  When Exme revived him --- using the healing shot she had intended for Delilah --- he looked at her with an expression she described as ``such an intense mixture of rage and disappointment'' and told her, coldly, ``This is all your fault.''  The words destroyed her.  She left the group shortly afterwards, and when she finally rejoined weeks later in Linderdorf, the two had to slowly rebuild their trust through the shared purpose of the final campaign.  By the assault on Seven's Spire, Exme's unsent letters to Myron suggested the feelings had not died --- only grown more complicated.

Myron developed a deep mutual respect with Riphard, the two bonding over their shared commitment to getting the job done and a shared fury after Delilah's murder.  In the aftermath, Myron carried Delilah's headless corpse back through the sewers, and he and Riphard privately agreed that Burnie --- a former Book Burner --- would have to die, though they never followed through.

His relationship with Burnie was initially hostile.  As a former Arbiter who participated in the Book Burner purge, Myron was deeply suspicious of Burnie and pelted the newcomer with bottles upon his arrival.  Over time, the two developed an uneasy respect.  When Burnie pressed him on where he stood, Myron stammered out a rare confession that Burnie was right about the nature of Kolo's death.  Their bond solidified through the final campaign, fighting side by side through Seven's Spire.

Martin began teaching Myron vocabulary in his sleep, steadily improving his eloquence --- a fact that became apparent during his rousing ``Emancipation'' speech in the Linderdorf arena.

\subsubsection{Myron's Story}

Myron proved himself immediately with the hunt for the Lord of Lightning --- a terrifying electric beast that had been killing balloon cows across the Masudan countryside.  While others prepared with ``a variety of levels of effectiveness, coolness and cowardice'', Myron slid underneath the creature, slashing and blasting its underbelly, emerging unscathed while the beast tried and failed to land a hit on him.

During the castle infiltration to rescue the nobleman's son Romjiro, Myron threw the Emperor's daughter Julieto out of a window --- and then fell out himself, temporarily dying before being revived.  His pragmatic approach to impossible situations made him the group's most reliable asset in their darkest hours.

When the party entered the Tom'ardy Mountains in search of Excalibrum, Myron accompanied them into the deep dwarven mines.  He survived gas traps, animated statues, and a near-fatal fall before being turned to stone by a pair of Gorgons alongside Kolo.  He remained petrified for weeks while the rest of the party pressed on, finally being destoned after the Excalibrum cache was discovered.

The worst came in Hope's Rest.  When Kolo --- corrupted beyond recognition by the elder entity Kalimar --- emerged in the sewers with his monstrous servants, Myron was the first to charge in, taking arrow after arrow as he fought to reach his friend.  He went down hard.  Exme revived him, but the sight of Delilah's crushed body and the children in danger drove Myron to cold fury.  He attempted to execute the subdued Kolo, and when Exme tried to stop him, he turned on her with the words that would haunt them both: ``This is all your fault.''  He carried Delilah's corpse back through the sewers without another word.

In the aftermath, Myron drowned his sorrows at the airship bar on a cocktail of his own invention --- the ``Piece of Shit'' --- and plotted with Riphard to kill Burnie, though neither followed through.  He wrote a letter to Rolltop Kandian warning the police chief about the Inquisitors, and threw bottles at Kevin's head when the young assassin boarded the ship.

The group's arrival in Linderdorf marked a turning point.  Myron visited his father, Professor Jorge Myron, at the university and met his stepmother Holly Dew for the first time --- learning that his own mother had joined a cult that was subsequently destroyed by the Church he served.  The reunion gave him some measure of peace.

At the ancient Bright Bart Forge high in the mountains, the party fought the Aspect of the Storm --- an air elemental wielding a lightning sword --- and claimed the forge's treasures.  Myron received Godbringer, an Excalibrum-forged weapon of devastating power.  He put it to immediate use in the Linderdorf arena, where the party challenged the Stonecast Mech --- a thirty-foot stone fighting robot.  Myron climbed the machine's legs under heavy fire, launched himself into the air, and brought Godbringer down in a final strike that bisected the robot entirely.  As the stadium erupted, Myron took a microphone from STANRI's speaker and delivered a rallying speech about the Church's tyranny and the coming liberation.  When Martin supplied the word Myron was reaching for, three-quarters of the crowd joined the chant: ``EMANCIPATION!''

Before the final assault on Seven's Spire, Myron walked alone into the hills above the festival town.  He found a spring beneath an overhanging tree, stacked pebbles by the water, drew the symbol of Synne in charcoal on the stones, and lit eight fires of different coloured oils.  He muttered a prayer to all the gods, asking for their guidance.  He received an immense feeling of hope and peace --- something he was not familiar with --- as the sun rose and cascaded across the land behind him.

At a coconut shy during the Festival of Light, a mysterious old man who seemed to know everything about Myron engaged him in cryptic conversation, hinting that he knew how things would turn out.  Myron threw a coconut, missed badly, threw again, won, and received a wink from the stranger.

Inside the Spire, Myron led from the front --- interrogating Lillith for tactical intelligence, bluffing guards with mixed success, and cutting through the tower's defenders floor by floor.  He was briefly killed by an Inquisitor's touch before being revived once again.  When the party found the prison level, it was Myron who pushed ahead alone, opening the heavy steel door to find Arbiter Kiros --- his former colleague --- strung up on a rack, barely alive.  He pulled out a fistful of cured beef jerky and stuffed it into her mouth, the pure essence of his will seemingly infusing the dried meat with enough strength to bring her back from the edge.  When Martin tried to kill the revived Kiros, Myron pinned him to the ground.

As the party donned Inquisitor disguises and prepared to ascend toward the pinnacle and the Great Stone, Myron declared that Pope Ezekiel the Third was going to pay for everything they had witnessed in those cells.
