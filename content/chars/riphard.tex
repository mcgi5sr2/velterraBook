\subsubsection{Background}

A dwarven pastor from the south, Riphard was killed by a member of the Inquisition before being resurrected by Lazarus and recruited into the mission to break the seal of the Seven.  His family, the Hardstones (or Hardthrusts), are an old dwarven dynasty from the Tom'ardy Mountains, though Riphard had long since left home to pursue his pastoral calling.

Before his death, Riphard was a man of the Church --- a true believer who preached the word of the Seven.  His encounter with Hell and subsequent resurrection shattered his faith and set him on a path of righteous vengeance against the very institution he once served.

\subsubsection{Personality and Traits}

Riphard is a hard-drinking, gun-toting dwarf with a talent for entrepreneurship and a flair for the dramatic.  Known for his thunderous whip attacks, his beloved firearms, and his tendency to launch into inspired sermons at the most inappropriate moments, Riphard is simultaneously the group's moral compass and its loosest cannon.

Despite his gruff exterior, Riphard has shown genuine tenderness --- his laying of hands on the fallen Otoria lasted ``a little too long'', and his tears upon learning he could train with the masters at the Sanctuary of Novetta were ``genuinely touching''.  He is fiercely protective of his companions, even if he would never admit it.

Riphard is also a shrewd businessman.  He founded the first bank in Velterra --- Northern Rock --- out of an old man's house in Riverfall, and later established the Guild of Banking in Hope's Rest.  His sales pitch for ``Safe Storage for a Dangerous World'' convinced even the sceptical Guild of Coin to invest.

\subsubsection{Relationships}

Riphard married Delilah Hardstone (n\'{e}e of the Jennies), a former courtesan turned crack shot and Sky Captain of Tomorrow.  The two had twin children --- Bilbo and Dildo --- whose births were attended by Anthony Tigerius and a squad of midwives from the Guild of Midwifery that Riphard had been bankrolling for exactly this purpose.  A faint golden light shone in the babies' eyes, hinting at a divine inheritance.

His relationship with the goblins was always complicated.  An admitted anti-goblin racist early on, Riphard gradually developed a grudging respect for the twins, particularly Exme's engineering genius and Kolo's tactical cunning --- though he and Kolo clashed frequently over business ethics, lemon theft, and the proper treatment of prisoners.  This grudging respect did not survive Kolo's corruption.

Riphard's childhood best friend Dagenham was found working as a blacksmith among the Delvers in the Tom'ardy mines.  The two rekindled their friendship immediately, only for Dagenham to die in a methane explosion shortly after --- a loss that hit Riphard hard enough for him to request Lazarus keep Dagenham's soul waiting in limbo for as long as possible.

Riphard earned the respect of Arbiter Kiros, who recognised his potential and recommended him for advanced training.  He also formed an unlikely bond with Pilcheur, the two sharing quiet moments of mutual understanding between bouts of mutual suspicion.  His competitive ping pong matches with Myron during the Masuda campaign were among the few moments of genuine camaraderie the dwarf allowed himself.

\subsubsection{Riphard's Story}

Riphard's journey took him from resurrection in Lazarus's office to becoming one of the most influential figures in Velterra.  He helped clear the Temple of Unthala, survived the Die Hard heist at the Tower of Imota Kan, founded a banking empire, crossed the Great Expanse, and fought alongside his companions through the jungles of South Africa and the civil war of Masuda.

In Masuda, Riphard acquired the legendary Hammer of the Gods from the Golden Hall --- an ancient weapon that turned out to be a gun firing beams of pure iridescent light.  His return to the Tom'ardy Mountains revealed the ugly truth of dwarven society: a classist, slave-dependent economy built on the backs of ``Delvers'' --- miners sent on one-way trips into the deep.  His own uncle, Targon Hardthrust, had become one of them.

In the deep mines beneath Tom'ardy, the group searched for Excalibrum --- the only metal capable of harming the Church's Inquisitors.  Riphard reunited with his uncle Targon, who had been searching for the ancient Hardstone family vault, only to watch helplessly as the feral Kolo murdered the old dwarf before his eyes.  Riphard and Myron were both turned to stone by Gorgons in the vault, only to be destoned later by Delilah and the group's potions.

The discovery of Excalibrum and the return to Hope's Rest brought both joy and devastation.  Delilah gave birth to the twins, and for a brief moment things seemed bright.  But Kolo resurfaced, monstrously mutated by Kalimar's corruption, and in a savage attack in the sewers beneath Hope's Rest, he crushed Delilah's skull.  In response, Riphard wrapped his whip around Kolo's throat and strangled the life from him, shouting the name ``Dagenham" as he pulled it tight.

The loss of Delilah broke something fundamental in Riphard.  By the time the group reached Tom'ardy again to rally allies, he had descended into delusion --- keeping a mannequin as a replacement wife and two sacks of rotten pork that he insisted were his children.  Despite this madness, Riphard remained a devastating combatant, killing an Inquisitor with the Excalibrum pickaxe and felling the Aspect of the Storm during the expedition to Bright Bart Forge in the Varg lands.

As the campaign reached its climax, Riphard stood with his companions at the Battle of Hope's Rest and the assault on Seven's Spire, driven not by faith or profit but by the simple dwarven stubbornness that had carried him from a polished stone room in Hell to the pinnacle of the Church's power.

\begin{DndSidebar}{Northern Rock PLC}
Riphard's banking empire began in Derek Bobacious's house in Riverfall with the slogan ``Safe Storage for a Dangerous World''.  By the time the group returned to Hope's Rest after the Masuda campaign, Northern Rock had expanded into multiple branches, generated enormous profits, and attracted investment from the Guild of Coin itself.  Riphard briefly considered diversifying into restaurants (``Hope's Chest'', staffed by buxom women) and acquired a schnitzel business in Linderdorf, though neither venture matched the success of the original bank.
\end{DndSidebar}
