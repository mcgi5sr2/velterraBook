It looked like he was doing some kind of alchemy. Myron picked up something that he was convinced was marmalade. We talked him down through some reverse reverse psychology. I introduced the team to Krankl. Myron asked him if he knew anything about the Kalimel™. It was story time. Myron asked him where dogs go when they die. The answer was apparently Krankl’s stomach. Ridiculous. The Kalimel comes from Kalimar himself. Valkarr is on the way to the source. Krankl told me I was his second favourite and that he always liked me even though he told me everyday that we were together that I deserved to die. Valkarr moved him here to teach him a lesson and make sure his research stayed secret. What research you ask? Valkarr never liked the gods so its strange he made an alliance with this new old god. “Creation does not mean you owe your life to them”. Valkarr made a deal with Kalimar to get power to take down the south but he also wanted an insurance policy. That’s where Krankl comes in, to study the goo and make a modified version of the goo that they can control. The madman. There are some things that goblins were not meant to mess with. I saw what this can do to Kolo first hand.\medskip

We argued about whether or not Krankls meddling was a good idea. I recommended we destroy everything. These kinds of things never go well. Erin was actually not unconvinced that this was a bad idea. I found this a bit strange. We wondered about these helper goblin monsters Krankl had made. Despite having two mouths they had been engineered to not speak so as to not reveal secrets. They did not look so happy. You know what they say “loose tongues, well hung”. Myron had the solution. We need him to not make more of this homebrew goo. We need him to make an antidote to the Kalimel. Krankl made his offer, 1000 gp, no goblin assistants, and women. He pointed at his wall. Dark haired white women. We offered him protection on the ship and a lab to make antigoo, with gold and dark haired white women consent pending. But how to get him to the ship. Erin said we needed to leave him here. This was not good, the gobblin is a liar and a cheat and he’ll betray us the moment we’re out of sight.\medskip

We asked him if he knew how we could find and track Valkarr, and he was like smirking a bit so we thought he knew more. Myron tried to get the truth out of him but he just laughed at him. Burnie tried to intimidate him. Krankl was not threatened but out of pity started to tell us what we wanted to know. But at the same time Riphard tried to mind control him to sign a contract and Erin simultaneously offered him a kingdom full of pornography. The porn won out when he found out that white haired dark women existed. The cat was out of the bottle and Krankl was furious at me. Well done Erin. What’s going on with her today? He reached into a drawer and threw me a shoddy little bundle of wires and chalk that he described as “a tracking device” for a tracker he had installed on Valkarr. Incredible. It wasn’t too hard to make it work. With this it was time to go since no-one else seemed to care that Krankl’s work here had at least a 34.67\% chance of destroying the bio-disc. We wondered about what a book is. Is a pub sign a book? Is a car with writing on it something that needs to be burned. Who can know these things? Burnie says that when in doubt it is just safer to burn everything. Riphard decided that one day he will make an asbestos book to annoy Burnie.\medskip

I sat in the back having a smoke with Krankl while everyone debated books and stuff. Burnie made an erotic statue out of stone. It was a lady that looked like myron and lady body but also dick. This was a strange mess. I told Krankl that Kalimar had Kolo. He said he didn’t care. I know he did. Kolo was his least favourite student. We left. I hate that goblin so much.\medskip

We left the temple and snuck out into the ‘streets’ of the inner varghold, leaving the town and resting. We followed the tracker device Northwards.\medskip

As the team marched North, Valkarrs army started their long slow march South. The invasion had started. The clock was ticking. Time was running out. Tick tock. Deadline looming. The sand was pouring into the bottom of the hourglass. The sun was rising/setting. Time.\medskip

Myron put his nose into the snow. We were a few days behind though the tracks were hard to make out. As we made our way North we found remnants of camps and other signs as we went along. Two days to be precise, an army with a size we could not determine. Maybe 2 -15. Myron set up a nice igloo for us but then lit a fire in it that was madness. Burnie got wet and smelled.\medskip
