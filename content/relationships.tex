\section{Character Relationships}

\noindent
Over a hundred and eight episodes, the bonds between the party members and the people around them shaped every decision, betrayal, sacrifice, and act of kindness in the campaign.  This section maps the connections that mattered most.

\subsection*{Romances}

\subsubsection*{Exme \& Myron}

The campaign's defining ``will they, won't they.''  Exme documented it in a self-published novelette, \textit{Love Doesn't Have a Height Limit}.  Their connection deepened through Masuda and the Linderdorf campaign, survived petrification and separation, but nearly shattered in the Hope's Rest sewers when Myron told her ``This is all your fault'' after Kolo killed Delilah.  She left the group; her unsent letters during her absence proved the feelings never died.  By the continuation they had adopted David together and ran the school side by side, but Exme's erasure from reality ensured the relationship was never resolved.

\subsubsection*{Riphard \& Delilah}

Riphard married the former courtesan turned crack shot and Sky Captain of Tomorrow.  He bankrolled the Guild of Midwifery for the express purpose of delivering their twins.  A faint golden light shone in the babies' eyes.  When Kolo crushed Delilah's skull in the sewers, Riphard strangled his old rival to death shouting the name of another friend the world had taken from him.  The loss broke him: by Tom'ardy he was keeping a mannequin wife and two sacks of rotten pork he insisted were his children.

\subsubsection*{Daisuke \& Archibald}

The Masudan interpreter and the Hope's Rest administrator found each other during the Masuda campaign.  Their romance epilogue in Episode 50 was one of the few moments of unambiguous happiness in the entire campaign.  Daisuke was later sacrificed to Kalimar by the corrupted Kolo.

\subsubsection*{King Oceani \& Lady Gharbighast}

The singing fool of the merfolk and the ruler of New Abbergast.  He fathered a child with her.  Oceani committed his entire army to the Battle of Hope's Rest and died in the fighting, his ghost rallying the troops for one final charge.

\subsubsection*{Mark \& Meredith}

She stood him up on prom night.  He spent three arcs being bitter about it.  They reconciled eventually.

\subsubsection*{Burnie \& Trayvon}

The details are sparse.  The result was Buck McCaw: half-human, half-Tengu, hideously disfigured, and raised by assassins.  Burnie abandoned them both.

\subsection*{Rivalries \& Antagonisms}

\subsubsection*{Kolo \& Riphard}

The campaign's longest-running feud.  Kolo stole from Riphard constantly, mocked his height, and took every opportunity to undermine the dwarf's authority.  Riphard responded with grudging tolerance that hardened into cold fury over time.  Every slight accumulated into a debt that Riphard finally collected in the sewers of Hope's Rest, wrapping his whip around Kolo's throat while shouting Dagenham's name.

\subsubsection*{Kolo \& Pilcheur}

Gleeful, one-sided torment.  Kolo called him ``Pilchard'' or ``Fishy,'' stole fifteen gold from him within hours of meeting, and never stopped.  Pilcheur endured it with the weary resignation of a man who had lost control of his own narrative.

\subsubsection*{Myron \& Burnie}

An Arbiter and a Book Burner, natural enemies by profession.  Myron pelted Burnie with bottles upon his arrival and maintained suspicion for weeks.  The two developed an uneasy respect forged through shared combat.  After Delilah's murder, Myron and Riphard privately agreed Burnie would have to die, though neither followed through.  When Burnie pressed Myron on where he stood, the gnome stammered out a rare confession that Burnie was right about the nature of Kolo's death.  Their bond solidified through Seven's Spire.

\subsubsection*{Otoria \& Pilcheur}

Otoria once pinned Pilcheur against a wall with her greatsword after he murdered a prisoner.  ``You will not do that again.''  She was the group's moral authority, and he was the group's most morally questionable member.  The tension was productive.

\subsubsection*{Lazarus \& Arbigal}

An office rivalry sustained across dimensional planes.  Lazarus ran Hell with catty disdain for his colleague Arbigal, whose competence threatened his authority and whose presence he tolerated only because the bureaucracy demanded it.

\subsection*{Deep Bonds}

\subsubsection*{Gary \& Kolo}

The pacifist stone elemental and the knife-wielding goblin who wanted every human dead.  They shared philosophical conversations about business, life, and goodness, even while Kolo kept trying to get Gary addicted to various substances.  Gary sacrificed himself to protect Kolo in the jungles of South Africa, standing tall as TikkiTukks hacked him to pieces.

\subsubsection*{Exme \& S.T.A.N.R.I}

More than creator and creation.  STANRI evolved from a leaking robo-cub to a mecha-battlesuit, each iteration reflecting Exme's growth as an inventor and her grief for fallen friends (the tiger redesign honoured Toni).  After Exme's erasure, STANRI took over as headmaster of her school.

\subsubsection*{Burnie \& Kevin}

Mentor and protege in an ``unhealthily abusive'' dynamic.  Buck McCraw introduced Kevin, a final-year PhD student from the University of Assassins, and Burnie took the young assassin under his wing with all the warmth of a man who had already failed at fatherhood once.

\subsubsection*{Mark \& Gary}

Gary acted as a father figure to Mark, the fragment of a god trapped inside mortal bodies.  ``Possibly the son of Gary (Peace be Upon Him),'' as the party records put it.

\subsubsection*{Myron \& Riphard}

Deep mutual respect forged through shared pragmatism.  They bonded over competitive ping pong in Masuda, mutual fury after Delilah's murder, and a shared willingness to get the job done.  Myron carried Delilah's headless corpse back through the sewers.  The two conspired to kill Burnie, and neither could go through with it.

\subsubsection*{Martin \& Burnie}

The construct became most attached to the gruff old Book Burner, who fixed his stutter, recharged him in the field, and treated him as a capable ally.  Martin's admiration was total; Burnie's acceptance was reluctant but genuine.
