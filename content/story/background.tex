\section{Background}

\subsection*{About This Book}

\noindent
This book is a record of the Velterra campaign, a Dungeons \& Dragons 5th Edition adventure that ran across two campaigns: eighty sessions between 2017 and 2019, and a twenty-eight session continuation in 2022, all run by Dungeon Master Michael Williams.  Six players guided their characters through a hundred and eight episodes of intrigue, violence, tragedy, and baked goods across a homebrew world where the gods had been sealed away and the Church that once served them had become the greatest threat to mortal souls.

The episode write-ups that form the bulk of this book were written by the players themselves, during or immediately after each session.  They are informal, irreverent, often profane, and frequently hilarious.  They have been preserved exactly as written, spelling mistakes, in-jokes, and all, because they capture the experience of the campaign far better than any polished retelling could.  The character pages, world entries, and this overview were written after the fact to provide context, but the episodes are the real story.

\subsection*{The World}

Velterra is a world shaped by faith.  Seven gods once walked among mortals, fought the forces of darkness, and taught their followers the virtues of justice, charity, strength, and hope.  Their Church grew powerful, its hierarchy of Pastors, Arbiters, and Inquisitors enforcing divine law across the known world.

But centuries ago, the Church performed the Sundering, using the Great Stone to sever the connection between the gods and the mortal plane.  What was meant as protection became a prison.  The gods did not abandon the world; they were locked out of it.  In their absence, the Church claimed sole authority over divine power, filtering what little remained through the Great Stone for its own purposes.  Its definition of sin expanded relentlessly, sending more and more innocent souls to Hell.

In Hell, the bureaucracy is drowning.  The flood of undeserving damned has overwhelmed the filing systems, the torture schedules, and the patience of one mid-ranking devil in particular.

His name is Lazarus.  And he has a plan.

\subsection*{The Mission}

\begin{DndReadAloud}
``You have all recently died.  You have been brought together, here, and given a second chance at life in exchange for carrying out a mission to destroy the Church.''

{\itshape\hfill Lazarus, to the newly resurrected party}
\end{DndReadAloud}

Lazarus reaches into the mortal world and plucks five recently deceased souls from the edge of oblivion.  He offers them a deal: a second chance at life, in exchange for breaking the seal that imprisons the gods and dismantling the corrupt Church that created it.  The group must first retrieve the Stone of Unthala, an ancient artefact that will serve as their means of communication with Hell.  Beyond that, the specifics are left to them.

They are warned not to attract the attention of the Church.

They will not be good at this.

\noindent
The party assembles in a featureless stone room in Hell, none fully understanding what has brought them there.  An elephantine creature named Meredith beckons them through a mysteriously appearing door into a plush office where a red-skinned man wearing a human face mask welcomes them.

\textbf{Pilcheur Gamont}, a cloaked human Book Burner, one of the Church's literary enforcers, tasked with hunting and destroying heretical texts.  Cowardly, scheming, and harbouring dark secrets.  Last seen fleeing across rooftops in Hope's Rest with a half-burnt tome under one arm before his pursuers caught up with him.

\textbf{Kolo and Exmerah Sliokzog}, twin goblin siblings.  Kolo is boisterous, light-fingered, and passionate about goblin emancipation by any means necessary.  Exme is softly spoken, fiercely intelligent, and already carrying the components for devices that will reshape the campaign.  They died fleeing the Varg in the frozen north, surrounded by brutal tribesmen.

\textbf{Riphard Obsidian Hardstone}, a dwarf pastor, a gun-toting man of the cloth whose faith was about to be shattered by the revelation that everything he believed in was built on a lie.  Shot dead in his own study by a figure who should not have existed.

\textbf{Lady Otoria Hearthrust}, a mysterious swordswoman from the distant kingdom of Nanduan.  Principled, lethal, and harbouring a secret that would only be revealed after her death: she was not human at all, but a dwarven-crafted automaton, a robot with a soul.  She died with honour, outnumbered by scores, choosing the only option she knew.

These five strangers have nothing in common except that they are all dead, all desperate, and all willing to make a deal with a devil.

\section{The Campaign}

\begin{multicols}{2}

\noindent
What follows is an overview of the campaign in sixteen arcs: eleven covering the original campaign from resurrection to the Seven's Spire, and five from the continuation that followed four years later.  For the full story in the players' own words, see the episode write-ups in Chapter 5.

\subsection*{Arc I: The Stone of Unthala}
\textit{Episodes 1--3}

\medskip

\noindent
The five strangers accept Lazarus's deal and are transported to a snow-bound cave in the southern wilderness with basic supplies and a simple objective: retrieve the Stone of Unthala from a temple to the south.  After two days of walking through frozen wasteland, during which Kolo relieves Pilcheur of fifteen gold pieces and the goblin twins disappear to drunkenly announce their adventurous intentions to an entire bar, the group arrives at the small settlement of Vathos Boundary.

South of the settlement lies a skull-shaped ice temple, home to the TikkiTukks, small bear-like creatures with face masks and sharp teeth.  The party navigates dart traps, fights masked bears and a giant spider, and retrieves the Stone of Unthala from the chief's quarters.  Kolo demonstrates his ability to speak the TikkiTukk language, a skill nobody expected him to possess, and Otoria establishes herself as the group's moral authority by refusing to tolerate cruelty of any kind.

The Stone secured, the party has its lifeline to Lazarus and its first taste of working together, a taste that leaves mixed impressions for everyone involved.  Nobody trusts anyone.  The party dynamic is already a disaster.

\subsection*{Arc II: Riverfall}
\textit{Episodes 4--9a}

\medskip

\noindent
Travelling north, the party encounters the Jennies, a criminal organisation of courtesans driven underground by the Church.  Among them is Delilah, an expert marksman who will become Riphard's wife, mother to his children, and ultimately one of the campaign's greatest losses.

In Riverfall, the group exorcises Pilcheur of a dark possession using pastoral chanting, Killepitsch liquor, and copious vomiting.  The shadow sword Black Sabbath manifests from Pilch's body.  Riphard founds the group's first bank, Northern Rock, in the house of an elderly bachelor named Derek Bobacious.  Then everything goes sideways.

A mercenary named Hans seizes the Tower of Imota Kan in what the group will forever call the ``Die Hard'' heist.  Across three sessions of infiltration, combat, and escalating chaos, the party fights through the tower floor by floor.  On the final night, a singing swordswoman named Lark drives her katana through Lady Otoria Hearthrust.

Otoria dies.  The party discovers, in the aftermath, that she was a robot, a dwarven-crafted automaton with a soul.  Around a campfire, they mourn the fiercest and most principled among them.  Kolo, unable to process the grief, begins telling a story.

\begin{DndSidebar}{In Memoriam}
The campaign claimed many lives.  The following is a record of every player character death, departure, and erasure:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ep 9a}: Lady Otoria killed by Lark's katana during the Die Hard heist
  \item \textbf{Ep 24}: Pilcheur Gamont dies at sea, cause uncertain
  \item \textbf{Ep 26}: Gary sacrifices himself protecting Kolo in the jungle, becomes a tree
  \item \textbf{Ep 33-5d}: Vu Dong erased from the timeline by the butterfly effect
  \item \textbf{Ep 54}: Kolo sacrifices Daisuke to Kalimar in the mines
  \item \textbf{Ep 66}: Anthony ``Toni'' Tigerius III departs the group, heading for the Expanse
  \item \textbf{Ep 68}: Kolo kills Delilah.  Riphard kills Kolo.  Exme leaves the group.
  \item \textbf{Ep 97}: Exmerah erased from existence by the Wish
  \item \textbf{Ep $\sim$100}: Riphard departs, investigating with the Jennies
  \item \textbf{Ep 101}: Sgt Erin Devereux, brain devoured by an Ulitharid
  \item \textbf{Ep 103}: Burnie charges the elder brain alone and is torn apart
\end{itemize}

Stephen Harland holds the record for most characters played (four).  Jonathan Mann's Exmerah survived the original campaign without dying or departing, only to be erased from existence in the continuation.
\end{DndSidebar}

\subsection*{Arc III: Hope's Rest}
\textit{Episodes 10--18}

\medskip

\noindent
The party arrives in Hope's Rest, the largest city in Velterra, a sprawling metropolis governed by five guild families controlling the domains of Life, Might, Coin, Service, and Making.  Here, the campaign shifts from wilderness survival to urban politics.

The group deals with the SRA, gnome and halfling terrorists led by a gnome named Razzle Backshine, whom they kill and replace with a more amenable successor called Buttons.  In Logarsk, they discover that the Church's Marshall has been using the Slab of Inthun to experiment on children in the church basement, and they encounter their first Arbiter: the terrifying plate-armoured Kiros, who will become one of their most important allies.

Back in Hope's Rest, Gary, the ancient stone elemental who awakened after Otoria's death, delivers an impassioned speech to the guild council that wins the group the right to establish their own guild.  The Gary Guild (``Garys Against Religion, Yo'') is born, operating from the abandoned Book Burner headquarters.  Riphard establishes a proper bank.  Gary joins the local police force as Officer Djago.  For a brief, shining moment, things almost seem to be going well.

\subsection*{Arc IV: Desert \& Sea}
\textit{Episodes 19--24}

\medskip

\noindent
The party crosses the Great Expanse, a vast desert where they forge an alliance with the Spancers, anti-Church bandits who will prove loyal friends.  From Port Averdale, a colourful port town of shady dealings, they set sail for the southern continent.

At sea, Pilcheur Gamont dies.  The group's original Book Burner, whose cowardice masked a complicated and ultimately tragic relationship with the dark forces possessing him, is gone.  From somewhere unexpected, Gary's chest, a golden-haired small figure emerges.  He calls himself Mark O'Synne, claims to be an avatar of the god Synne, and is immediately and universally disliked by everyone.

\subsection*{Arc V: The Jungle Continent}
\textit{Episodes 25--28}

\medskip

\noindent
The party arrives on the jungle continent via the Caribbean-like port of Akultheok.  The jungles are dangerous, cannibal TikkiTukks, hostile wildlife, and worse.  Deep in the undergrowth, they discover the Tower of Ruh'Breks, the hidden underground stronghold of an ancient sorcerer.

Ruh'Breks himself, dying and centuries old, gifts the party two things that will define the rest of the campaign: the Puzzle Die, a magical cube of seemingly infinite functions and semi-sentient intelligence, and an airship powered by spectral energy batteries, ghost-like entities captured in storage compartments.  The party names the airship after Gary, who sacrifices himself in the jungle protecting Kolo from danger.  Gary transforms into a tree, destined to regenerate in several hundred years.

The gnome Arbiter Myron arrives via hot air balloon.  The monkey companion Vu Dong joins from the jungle.  The group is airborne, and the campaign enters a new phase.

\subsection*{Arc VI: Masuda}
\textit{Episodes 29--48, including flashbacks 33.5a--33.5d}

\medskip

\noindent
The longest arc of the campaign takes the party to the foreign kingdom of Masuda, a land of samurai culture torn apart by civil war.  What begins as a simple quest to retrieve the Hammer of the Gods, a legendary weapon said to have defeated an Inthun-infused Varg in a single blow, becomes a sprawling adventure of political intrigue, battlefield chaos, and unexpected alliances.

The group becomes Ronin, hunts the Lord of Lightning, infiltrates a castle, and forges an alliance with the Hearthrust Society, a movement founded by Otoria herself before she came to Velterra, dedicated to the emancipation of robot ladies.  Lady Hirota Hearthrust rescues the party with a mechanical eagle.  The Hammer of the Gods is retrieved, and turns out to be an ancient gun that fires beams of pure iridescent light.

Midway through the Masuda campaign, the timeline fractures.  Mark O'Synne constructs a VR time-travel machine that allows the party to relive events from decades past, experiencing the world of the Book Burners firsthand.  Through the eyes of Burnie Cinders, a human raised by dwarven parents who survived the Book Burner purges, the party witnesses the training, the missions, and the catastrophic betrayal that destroyed the order.  The critical revelation: it was Pilcheur who caused the purge.  His habit of secretly selling books instead of burning them was discovered, and the Church used it as justification to wipe out every Book Burner alive.  The flashbacks end with Vu Dong erased from the timeline by the butterfly effect, and Burnie, now fully realised as a character, joining the main campaign permanently with a stone dagger, a half-Tengu son, and enough guilt for two lifetimes.

By the arc's end, the airship has been upgraded with cannons and weaponry, Ruh'Breks Sensei has died holding back a mountain creature to protect the party, and the group departs Masuda for the dwarven homeland with a growing sense that the endgame is approaching.

\subsection*{Arc VII: Tom'ardy Mountains}
\textit{Episodes 49--53}

\medskip

\noindent
The party returns to the dwarven homeland, Riphard's ancestral mountains, seeking Excalibrum, the legendary star-metal that is the only material capable of harming the Church's Inquisitors.  What they find is a society built on slavery.  The Tom'ardy dwarves maintain an economy of Delvers, miners sent on one-way expeditions into the deep, and the party's disruption of this system will have consequences that ripple through the rest of the campaign.

In the deep mines, Lillith, a half-elf Book Burner who ``actually liked Pilch'', is found hiding in a barrel.  Riphard's uncle Targon is discovered among the Delvers.  And somewhere in the darkness below, something ancient stirs.  The elder entity Kalimar, a being of terrifying power whose domain is the warping of flesh, has taken an interest in the party.  The mines are deeper than anyone thought, and each level brings the group closer to something they are not prepared for.

\subsection*{Arc VIII: Kolo's Descent}
\textit{Episodes 54--68}

\medskip

\noindent
This arc encompasses the campaign's darkest period.  In the deep mines, Kolo sacrifices the party's interpreter Daisuke to Kalimar in a ritual that marks the point of no return.  He disappears into the darkness.  Myron is turned to stone by Gorgons.  The party barely escapes with their lives.

A new character joins: Anthony K. Tigerius III, a large tiger-man sent from the Guild of Coin to negotiate about the bank.  He carries a briefcase and an unnecessary cane, gives powerpoint presentations, and flies into berserker rages at inconvenient moments.  While Toni negotiates merfolk trade deals and the party fights their first Inquisitor, discovering that these dark masked figures with tentacle maws are immune to every weapon except Excalibrum, the shadow of Kolo's corruption hangs over everything.

Exme rebuilds STANRI in tiger form, honouring Toni.  Myron is destoned.  Excalibrum is finally found in the mines.  The first Inquisitor is killed with an Excalibrum pickaxe, proving the metal works.  But it all leads to Hope's Rest, and the sewers beneath it.

Kolo is found.  Massively mutated, one giant arm, wings, covered in fungus, he has become Kalimar's servant, building a lair in the sewers beneath the city the party once called home.  Exme stops time to save babies from Kolo's spawn.  But she cannot save Delilah.  Kolo crushes the head of Riphard's wife.

\begin{DndReadAloud}
``Today is the day that we will have to kill Kolo.''

{\itshape\hfill Myron, Episode 68}
\end{DndReadAloud}

Riphard strangles Kolo to death with his whip.  Exme, broken by her brother's death at the hands of her companions, leaves the group.  She takes Kolo's body and departs with a note for Burnie: ``I thought I could have both of you.''  It is the lowest point of the campaign.

\subsection*{Arc IX: Aftermath \& The Forge}
\textit{Episodes 69--75}

\medskip

\noindent
The aftermath is bleak.  Kevin, a final-year PhD student from the University of Assassins with Christopher Mintz-Plasse energy, joins the group via Buck McCraw, Burnie's half-Tengu son.  The party visits Lazarus, who confirms Delilah's soul is in Hell.  Kolo's soul, however, is missing, claimed by Kalimar, beyond even the reach of devils.  Riphard begins his descent into delusion: a mannequin becomes Delilah, two sacks of rotten pork become his children.  Myron invents a cocktail, one part sherry, one part grenadine, one part Bovril, and names it the Piece of Shit after Riphard.

The group sets course for Linderdorf, a dense warren of foundries, mills, and shops built into a mountain.  Myron reunites with his father, Professor Jorge Myron.  The master blacksmith Google Von Maccherstein has gone exploring in the Varg lands and not returned.  The hunt for Google takes the party into frozen territory, where Martin Andleberger, a construct bard built to be a fighting butler, is found beaten and chained by Varg bearkin.  The party rescues Google, discovers the Bright Bart Forge, and defeats the Aspect of the Storm to retrieve the forging knowledge needed to work Excalibrum.

Back in Linderdorf, Google's team forges the weapons.  Chief among them: Godbringer, a weapon of immense divine power that Myron will wield in the battles to come.  Exme returns to the group and discovers that the Puzzle Die can locate the source of the seal, it points directly at Seven's Spire.

The arena fight at Clawtech provides the campaign's most triumphant moment.  Facing a thirty-foot Stonecast Mech, Myron launches himself into the air and brings Godbringer down in a final strike that bisects the robot entirely.  Three-quarters of the stadium erupts in chants of ``Emancipation.''  The party wins the giant robot.

\subsection*{Arc X: Alliance Building}
\textit{Episodes 76--78}

\medskip

\noindent
Exme plugs the Puzzle Die into the airship's ghost engine and achieves hyper-speed travel, pinning the crew against their seats and allowing the party to cross Velterra in days rather than weeks.  What follows is a whirlwind recruitment drive:

Tom'ardy, now a socialist utopia following the party's disruption of the slave economy, commits its dwarves to the fight.  Lillith convinces their council.  The merfolk of New Abbergast pledge their army.  Lady Gharbighast holds firm despite Church pressure.  The Jennies, the assassins, the Garys, the commoners of Hope's Rest, all are rallied.  Linderdorf sends robots and cannons.  The Hearthrust Society sends mechanical eagles piloted by female ninja riders.

Lillith infiltrates Seven's Spire disguised as a pastor during the Festival of Light, a pilgrimage celebration of all the gods that provides the perfect cover.  When the party arrives at the Spire's gates, Lillith has them ``arrested'' and marched inside.  They are in.

On a hilltop the night before, Myron prays to all seven gods.  He is an Arbiter of the Church, a warrior-enforcer who was once part of the system he is about to destroy.  He asks for strength.  The campaign is entering its final act.

\subsection*{Arc XI: The Battle \& The Spire}
\textit{Episodes 79--80}

\medskip

\noindent
The alliance army meets the Church's forces at the Battle of Hope's Rest.  Eight thousand Church troops face a coalition of dwarves, fishmen, clockwork ninjas, mechanical eagles, commoners, and Garys.  The casualties are heavy on both sides.  King Oceani dies singing a song, then rallies his troops as a ghost.  The Church eventually flees.

Inside Seven's Spire, the party fights upward floor by floor.  Burnie suffers a book-burning episode of mania, incinerating a library and its sherry-swilling occupant.  Martin fights inside the Stonecast Mech, dropped from the airship as a siege weapon.  The party discovers the prison level, corridors reeking of death and decay, cells containing tortured prisoners and desiccated corpses.

Myron finds Kiros.  The Arbiter who became their ally is strung up on a rack, naked and barely alive.  He stuffs beef jerky into her mouth.  She survives.

Arbiter Khan arrives with two Inquisitor Ravagers, massive hulking beings with metal spikes for hands.  Khan dances fluidly through the entire party.  Kiros unloads a full set of chambered rounds into him.  Then Exme's linear accelerator vaporises his head.  STANRI steps on the remains.

The party ascends toward the pinnacle of the Spire, where the Great Stone awaits.  The seal must be broken.  The gods must return.  And Kolo's soul, trapped with Kalimar, beyond the reach of devils, may only be saved by divine intervention.

The final confrontation, in which the party breaks the seal and becomes demigods, has yet to be written up.  The original campaign ended.  The story did not.

\end{multicols}

\section{The Continuation}

\begin{multicols}{2}

\noindent
Four in-world years after the Spire, the campaign resumed.  The table reconvened in 2022 with the original five players and one new addition, Natalie Southernden.  What followed was twenty-eight episodes of loss, sacrifice, and a heist.

\subsection*{Arc XII: One More Time}
\textit{Episodes 81--84}

\medskip

\noindent
Magic has returned to the world but the gods remain distant.  The scattered, half-broken party is regathered by Lazarus, now ruling Hell as the Empire of the Eight, alongside a new recruit: Sgt Erin Devereux, a career soldier from the western city of Jecede and sole survivor of a reconnaissance team sent into the Varg lands.

The world has changed.  Exme runs a school for gifted girls.  Riphard owns a mega-bank.  Burnie is collecting books instead of burning them.  Myron commands a troop of former Arbiters.  They are older, stranger, and in several cases not entirely sane.

A warm-up against the lich Valklondar reintroduces the party to the rhythms of violence, and the real threat is revealed: Valkar Varg has made a pact with Kalimar, and the elder god's flesh-warping goo is being used to brew super-soldiers.  The mission: stop the invasion, kill Kalimar, and rescue Kolo's soul.

\subsection*{Arc XIII: Among the Varg}
\textit{Episodes 85--92}

\medskip

\noindent
The party infiltrates the Varg lands and joins the Chesney Hawks, a barbarian tribe led by the giantess Magrat, who eats a man in a single bite.  They hunt cave mammoths and boreworms to earn respect.  Riphard kills a boreworm that has swallowed the half-giant Eric whole, and Martin is exploded into pieces by the snow leopard warrior Buchra during a sparring match that gets out of hand.

Valkar declares war and marches south with his Pact warriors.  The party recovers Krankle, the goblin twins' childhood tormentor, and sets him brewing an antidote to the Kalimel goo while they track Valkar north.  The frozen wastes grow darker.  The trail leads down.

\subsection*{Arc XIV: The Destruction of the Creator}
\textit{Episodes 93--97}

\medskip

\noindent
Down through an ice-cliff temple, ratman hives, fungal forests, and the flesh-tunnel bowels of a god, the party descends to Kalimar's lava sanctum.  The elder god of creation nearly wipes them.  Myron is stabbed through the chest and killed outright.  Burnie is swallowed whole, held alive inside the creature only by Synne's divine intervention.  Mrs Ball, the party's improbable ally, annihilates Kalimar with her biggest bonk.

Then Exmerah uses the Wish.

The Puzzle Die's wish function, the only wish in existence, ten-thousand-year cooldown, is spent on an impossible ask.  Myron is critically injured, paralysed, dying.  Kolo's soul has been trapped for four years.  Exme cannot choose between them, so she chooses both: her own life in exchange for Myron's healing and Kolo's restoration.  The wish is accepted, but the cost is absolute.  Exmerah Sliokzog is erased from existence, not dead but unmade, beyond resurrection, beyond godhood, beyond the reach of Heaven or Hell.  She is gone as though she never was.

Kolo wakes in a construct body built by his sister.  Myron opens his eyes.  Neither knows yet what it cost.

\subsection*{Arc XV: Canalon Descends}
\textit{Episodes 98--103}

\medskip

\noindent
Kalimar is dead.  But Kalimar had a mate.

Canalon, an elder-god space dragon, descends from the stars and mind-controls the entire allied army, seizing Hope's Rest to erase free will and destroy the gods.  The party, now including the devil Bronwen sent by Lazarus as a minder and Kolo in his new metal body, rallies the western carnival city of Jecede, Erin's home.

It is not enough.  A githyanki Nautiloid attacks the city.  The party boards it.  An Ulitharid mind flayer devours Erin's brain.  Myron takes her fallen glaive and splits the creature in two.

Two episodes later, Burnie dies.  The old Book Burner charges the Nautiloid's elder brain alone, buying the others time, and is torn limb from limb.  Captain Stick goes down with the Airship Gary, steering the burning vessel into the crashing Nautiloid to deflect it from the city.

Gem, a woman of living silicate freed from a holding cell aboard the wreckage, joins the survivors.  She has travelled between stars for five centuries.  Her people were created when Canalon was cast out of the heavens.  She has her own reasons for wanting the dragon dead.

\subsection*{Arc XVI: The Last Heist}
\textit{Episodes 104--108}

\medskip

\noindent
Myron leads what is left: himself, Kolo, Bronwen, Gem, and Bucky McCaw, Burnie's son.  They board a maiden-voyage steam railway from Jecede to Hope's Rest, fighting Inquisitors across the train roof.  Hope's Rest is a ghost city, its population mind-controlled, Canalon perched atop the guildmasters' hall.

The target is the Black Rock, Riphard's bank.  Its deepest vault holds the Armoury of Sin, the gear the god Sinn wore when he last fought Canalon.  Using a relief army as a distraction, the remnant party heists the deserted bank, fighting through bronze automaton guards and a teleporting, lightning-wreathed Arcanist in a bullet-time lobby battle.  They crack the vault with the code 51624 while its owner is somewhere else entirely.

The Armoury is claimed.  The written record ends at Episode 108.

\begin{DndReadAloud}
``Well, we're off to fight a God.''

{\itshape\hfill Episode 108}
\end{DndReadAloud}

The final battle with Canalon, wearing the armour of a god against a god, was never written up.  The players know what happened.  The book does not.  Perhaps some stories end better for not having an ending.

\end{multicols}

\begin{DndSidebar}{Themes of the Campaign}
Beneath the irreverence and the chaos, Velterra is a campaign about emancipation, of goblins, of gods, of people from corrupt institutions.  It is about found family and the cost of losing it.  It is about what happens when good people are given power by bad systems, and what it takes to tear those systems down.

It is also about a dwarf who married a prostitute, founded a bank, and then carried a mannequin around believing it was his dead wife.  It is about a goblin who ate a biscuit and discovered the joy of eating.  It is about a cocktail made with Bovril.

The campaign never chose between tragedy and comedy.  It was always both.

The continuation proved the point.  Four years later, the same group came back for more.  They lost more than they could afford to lose, and they kept going anyway.
\end{DndSidebar}
